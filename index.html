I use health data and epidemiology to find out what reduces disease — at scale.

I'm a physician-epidemiologist (MBBS, MPH) working on type 2 diabetes and other metabolic noncommunicable diseases — from pragmatic trials to simulation models.

At ICMR National Institute of Epidemiology in Chennai, I work across the full arc of a study — from the design question, through the analysis, to the effect estimate that's meant to change practice and policy.

The methods I reach for most are those for longitudinal and clustered data — mixed-effects models, missing-data methods, and causal inference — alongside evidence synthesis, worked almost entirely in R and Quarto. But the methods are a means, not the point: I came to epidemiology from clinical medicine, and I care as much about whether an intervention reaches people as about whether the estimate is unbiased.

Before research, I led a statewide type 1 diabetes care programme across all 14 districts of Kerala — which is where I learned that good evidence and good delivery are the same problem seen from two ends.
